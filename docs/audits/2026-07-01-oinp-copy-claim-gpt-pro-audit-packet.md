## Core Intent

The website should achieve three things:

1. Share Joe's perspective as a real example of a broader issue.
2. Raise public awareness in Canada's tech, startup, university, media, and policy-adjacent communities.
3. Invite simple public support and collect related stories from people affected by, or observing, the same pattern.

The secondary goal is to let the right people learn about Joe or contact him, but this must not dominate the page.

## Strategic Concern To Pressure-Test

The current page says Canada and Ontario should:

> Count builder evidence: shipped products, users, pilots, companies, research, teaching, and community contribution.

Joe is concerned this may be emotionally right but administratively unrealistic for IRCC or OINP. It may sound like asking immigration officers to evaluate startup traction directly, which could be difficult, subjective, or politically easy to attack.

Please audit whether the ask should be reframed toward a more realistic and policy-fitting claim, such as:

- keeping an independent graduate pathway for high-potential Masters/PhD talent;
- protecting students and graduates who planned around published pathways;
- recognizing that traditional employment is not the only way graduate talent contributes;
- asking Ontario/Canada to design a pathway, review mechanism, pilot, advisory process, nomination lane, or evidence framework that can recognize early-stage builder contribution without pretending every immigration officer can judge startup quality;
- using Joe's case to show why the old Masters/PhD-style independent pathway had value for early-stage builders because it did not force all contribution into one employer relationship too early.

If the current ask is too broad, please propose safer, sharper wording.

## Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Keep the site story-led and human. Do not turn it into a government PDF.
- Keep hero and display copy concise; the page is video-led and visually designed.
- Do not make the page sound like "Joe is the perfect example for policymakers." The point is one example of a broader pattern.
- Do not let hiring or "Build with Joe" become the main purpose.
- Avoid over-accusatory claims such as "Canada pushes builders out" unless carefully qualified.
- Use primary sources for any policy claim.
- Flag anything that may be legally/politically risky, misleading, or too absolute.
- If recommending links, provide source title, URL, exact claim supported, and page placement.
- If a source is weak, say it is weak.

Date: July 2, 2026

## Instruction For ChatGPT Pro

You are an adversarial external reviewer using ChatGPT Pro with extended thinking.

This is another follow-up for `https://oinp.hubeiqiao.com/`. We are close, but the latest copy still has several strategic problems. Please do not only polish sentences. Challenge the framing, information architecture, and section logic.

The site is a concise, video-led public-awareness page by Joe Hu. It uses Joe's story as one concrete example to raise awareness about whether Canada's immigration policy can attract and keep startup-era contributors who are already here, already building, and already becoming part of the local ecosystem.

## Core Goal

The goal is not to ask for job-offer relief.

Joe can find a job. The page should not make readers think he is asking for help because he cannot get employment.

The stronger point is:

Canada helped Joe become a builder. He studied here, built a product here, registered a company here, joined communities here, and entered the immigration process under a graduate pathway. Then Ontario changed the program and no further invitations will be issued under the former graduate streams.

This is one story, but the broader question is whether Canada and Ontario know how to design immigration policy that attracts and keeps the kind of people who create startup activity, products, companies, research, community energy, and future jobs before their value is easy to classify.

Current title:

> Bigger than one application

Current body:

Problem:

The title may be okay, but the "one permanent job offer" language is not okay. It makes the argument look employment-relief oriented.

### At a glance

Problems:

### Evidence/resources

> YC AI Startup School: Accepted into YC AI Startup School.

Problems:

- YC AI Startup School should show outside startup ecosystem signal, not just a label.

### The Ask

Current title:

> Keep a graduate pathway beyond one job offer

Current intro:

> Job offers matter. Official language ability matters. But early graduate contribution can begin as research, products, prototypes, teaching, company formation, or startup attempts before it becomes one permanent job. That is why an independent graduate path matters.

Ask 1:

> Protect people already on the path
>
> People made tuition, career, and life decisions around published graduate streams. When rules change mid-process, there should be a clear transition.

Ask 2:

> Keep an independent graduate path
>
> Ontario-trained Masters and PhD talent should have a route that can consider education, language, settlement intent, and potential without requiring one permanent employer relationship first.

Ask 3:

> Use builder activity as evidence
>
> Products, users, research, teaching, company formation, and ecosystem work should show why this path matters, not force officers to judge every startup directly.

Problems:

- The main title still over-centers "job offer."
- "Before it becomes one permanent job" is wrong.
- "Ontario-trained Masters and PhD talent" is too narrow if used as the whole frame.
- Ask 3 is weak. "Evidence" for what? Maybe this should become a startup-ecosystem point.
- The ask should be about rethinking immigration policy to attract and keep builders, while using the Masters/PhD stream as one important example of a stable alternative.

## The Strategic Direction To Test

Please test whether this is the right direction:

The page should say:

1. Joe's case is personal, but not only about him.
2. Canada helped him become a builder.
3. He is not asking for job relief.
4. He is asking whether Canada and Ontario can design immigration policy for people who are already creating value but whose value may not yet look like conventional employment, a regulated occupation, or a mature company.
5. The former Masters/PhD graduate pathway mattered because it gave highly educated talent a stable, non-employer-dependent alternative.
6. But the broader question is not only Masters/PhDs. It is Canada's ability to attract and keep early-stage builders, founders, researchers, product people, and community contributors in the AI/startup era.
7. Joe Speaking and YC AI Startup School should be proof that Joe is not abstractly claiming potential; he has built, shipped, attracted users, and joined startup circles.
8. The final ask should be constructive: protect transition fairness, keep independent graduate pathways, and design immigration policy that supports Canada's startup ecosystem.

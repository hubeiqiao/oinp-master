# GPT Pro Copy Audit Packet: OINP Awareness Website

You are an adversarial external copy strategist and public-awareness campaign reviewer using deep reasoning.

## Intent

Audit the copy for a public awareness website about Joe Hu's OINP / Ontario immigration policy story.

The page is not meant to be a formal petition, a policy memo, or a hiring page. It should persuade more people in Canada's tech, startup, university, and policy-adjacent communities to understand the story, care about fair pathways for builders already here, share the message, and optionally add public support or share their own story.

Core idea:

- Joe is one concrete example of the kind of person Canada says it wants: someone who studied here, built products here, registered a company here, joined communities here, and is already contributing.
- The story is personal, but it is not only about Joe.
- The bigger question is whether Canada and Ontario can recognize early-stage builders before they leave.
- The page should help move from "Joe's story" to a broader public-awareness frame and invite more examples from students, graduates, founders, builders, employers, professors, investors, and community members.

## Constraints And Non-Goals

- Preserve Joe's voice: personal, direct, slightly disappointed, builder-focused, not bureaucratic.
- Do not make the page sound like a generic policy memo.
- Do not over-center "policymakers" in a stiff way. Policymakers are one audience, but the broader audience is the Canadian tech/startup/university/policy community.
- Keep the primary hierarchy:
  1. Share Joe's perspective.
  2. Raise public awareness.
  3. Invite support and more stories.
  4. Keep "work with Joe" secondary.
- Avoid overclaiming. Be persuasive, but do not make unsupported legal or policy claims.
- Avoid language that sounds entitled, demanding, or anti-Canada.
- Avoid making the page mainly about finding Joe a job.
- The current design is cinematic/editorial. Copy should support that tone.
- Return only potential copy/content changes and strategy suggestions. Do not provide code.

## Audit Focus

Please audit for:

1. Persuasion: does the page make a sharp, memorable case?
2. Narrative arc: does it move well from Joe's story to the broader public issue?
3. The three asks: are they the right three points, and are they framed sharply enough?
4. Public awareness: does the site invite people to share, support, and contribute more examples?
5. Credibility: does the copy show Joe as a strong example without sounding self-congratulatory?
6. Policy framing: does it make the policy issue understandable without becoming a government PDF?
7. Social sharing: are title, description, share text, and CTA copy strong enough for LinkedIn/X/email?
8. Risk: any wording that may feel unsafe, exaggerated, politically clumsy, too harsh, or too weak?
9. Missing sections or missing proof points that would make the page more persuasive.
10. Concrete revisions: exact copy changes, ranked by impact.

## Required Output Format

Please return:

1. Verdict: READY / READY WITH COPY CHANGES / NEEDS STRATEGIC REVISION.
2. The top 5 copy changes to make before launch, ranked by persuasion impact.
3. Specific rewritten copy candidates for each recommended change.
4. Whether the three asks should be kept, changed, reordered, or reframed.
5. Suggestions for making the page collect more stories/examples without making the support flow heavy.
6. Social sharing improvements: title, meta description, OG description, LinkedIn/X/email share copy.
7. Copy risks or claims that should be softened or verified.
8. A short "launch-ready narrative spine" in 5-7 bullets.

Please be direct. If something is weak, say exactly why and propose sharper wording.

2. Should the page say more clearly that Joe is only one example and that the site is looking for more examples/stories?
3. Is the language "Canada makes world-class founders, then its rules push them out" too strong, or is it useful for social sharing?
4. Is "Keep builders in Canada" sharper than "fair pathways for builders already here," or does it sound too slogan-like?
5. Should the page include a small "what you can do" sequence: support, share, add your story, introduce this to someone in tech/university/policy?
6. Should the resource cards better establish credibility before the policy ask?
7. What is the strongest possible one-sentence thesis for this page?

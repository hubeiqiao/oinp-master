The full story

Bigger than one application

This is a 2-minute story about how Canada helped me become a builder, what shifted when the pathway changed, and why people already building here need fair ways to be recognized.

This page is personal, but the argument is not sentimental. Ontario redesigned the program, and the old pathways are gone. The question is whether Canada can recognize people already studying, building, and contributing here before they fit the shape of a traditional job.

The ask

Recognize contribution before it becomes conventional employment

A full-time job offer is one kind of evidence. French ability is one kind of evidence. But people also contribute through shipped products, users, pilots, research, teaching, company-building, and community work. If Canada wants builders, the evidence should include what they actually build.

  1. 01
    Planned pathways People made tuition, career, and life decisions around published pathways.

    Protect those who planned ahead

    Read full ask

    Current students and recent graduates should not be left in sudden uncertainty after making major tuition, career, and life decisions around pathways that existed when they chose Canada.

    Time-sensitive
  2. 02
    Independent graduate contribution Graduate talent does not always show up as one permanent employer relationship.

    Let graduates be recognized on their own merit

    Read full ask

    A system centered on employer demand can miss graduates contributing through contract work, startups, research collaborations, teaching, open-source work, and early customer pilots. Those signals should not disappear just because they do not fit one full-time permanent job offer.

    Structural
  3. 03
    Builder evidence Products, users, pilots, company registration, and community work are evidence.

    Count what builders actually build

    Read full ask

    Create a practical way to recognize early-stage builders already in Ontario by evidence of real traction: shipped products, users, pilots with Ontario firms, registered companies, research, revenue, investment, or community contribution.

    Opportunity
